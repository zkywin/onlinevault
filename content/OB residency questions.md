OB Senior Questions:  
  
1) If we are very interested in matching at LLU should we give a signal even though it is our home institution? Or should we use our signals elsewhere to increase our chances of getting an interview someplace else? How about gold vs silver?

Yes, if you are interested in your home institution, then you should signal your home institution.  This is consistent with the standards specialty-wide for OBGYN.  Signals should be commensurate with your interest in the program, i.e. if you are highly interested in your home program then you should send a gold signal, or if it is a preferred program then you should send a silver signal.    
  
2) If we don’t signal a particular program, does that mean we shouldn’t expect to get an interview at those places?

Data presented at the last annual CREOG meeting (Council on Residency Education in OBGYN) indicated the following:

Sending a gold signal meant applicants had a 62% probability of interview invitation

Sending a silver signal meant applicants had a 35% probability of interview invitation

Sending no signal meant applicants had a 3% probability of interview invitation

Take home message is your chance of getting an interview at a particular problem is markedly reduced if you do not signal that program

  
3) Dr. Codorniz mentioned IM programs like to see the experiences written in bullet point rather than paragraph form. Is this true for OB as well?

It is difficult to answer this question since we are not sure yet how programs will be able to view the applications in ResidencyCAS.

In ERAS, some people used bullet point and others used paragraph format.  I think it depends on personal preference of the applicant.  Both formats were used in ERAS for OBGYN.

  
4) For the presentation section of the application, what kinds of things are we allowed to include? How formal of a presentation does it need to be? Can we include informal teachings we did for our teams while on service, or does it have to be a true organized event?

Presentations generally refer to formal teaching that requires preparation and is given to a group of people.  Presentations at Grand Rounds or a noon conference or to your team could be included.  You would just need to specify what type of event it is in ResidencyCAS and I believe there is a space in the application to include the name of the event or meeting.

  
5) if we have ongoing research that will be submitted for publication/presentation later, can they be listed in the publication or presentation section? Or do they have to be in experiences?

I believe it would need to be in the experiences section.  Looking at the application worksheet, it appears there are only sections for published or submitted research.  So projects in progress would probably need to go under the experiences section.

  
6) How many programs should we be applying for if we are “average” in competitiveness? What’s the best way to determine how competitive a program is? Or how competitive *we* are?

This is highly individualized, I would recommend talking to Dr Hart about this.  

The OBGYN community introduced the ACI (Alignment Check Index) a few years ago through FREIDA as a way to look for programs that are aligned with your priorities.  Program websites can also be helpful.  Generally academic programs are considered more competitive than community-based programs but there are definitely exceptions.

  
7) What would you say is important when we’re comparing a program’s curriculum or structure? Are there any “must haves” in your opinion?

This also depends on your priorities.  If you are interested in pursuing a fellowship, then you want to consider programs that are more academic, that have a history of matching residents into fellowship and have options such as research rotations and/or elective rotations.  

If you are interested in underserved populations, then you want to consider a program that prioritizes this and has rotations in FQHC clincs or in county hospitals. 

If you are interested in global health, then you want to consider programs that have this option. 

Generally a program's website gives you a good idea of what each program emphasizes and what their mission statement is.  I would also recommend looking at the block schedule for each program (this is often posted on the website) to see what types of rotations the residents participate in.

  
8) Any recommendations for how many “out of region” places to apply to? Especially now that residencyCAS doesn’t have a region signal.

Data from the last few OBGYN application cycles has indicated that geographical preference carries significant weight for most applicants.  So, if you are interested in a particular area, then you would want to indicate why are you applying to this area in your application and/or your personal statement (i.e. Hometown, family in the area, significant other, etc...).  Generally if you are applying for an area to which you have no major ties, then you would want to indicate somewhere in your application why you are applying to that program—i.e. curriculum aligned with your personal interests, strong preparation for fellowships, etc...​​  Rember programs are always thinking about retaining residents for future faculty as well as training residents who have a high likelihood of completing the residency program.  Often programs will prioritize applicants who have some sort of local connection to the region.
