The ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 128 on diagnosing abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB) in reproductive-aged women provides guidelines using the PALM-COEIN classification system. Key points include:

1. **Classification System**:
    
    - [[AUB]] is classified into structural (PALM) and nonstructural (COEIN) causes:
        - **PALM**: Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma, Malignancy and Hyperplasia.
        - **COEIN**: Coagulopathy, Ovulatory dysfunction, Endometrial causes, Iatrogenic, and Not yet classified.
2. **Diagnosis and Evaluation**:
    
    - Diagnosis includes a thorough history, physical exam, and targeted laboratory testing (e.g., pregnancy test, CBC, TSH).
    - Imaging with transvaginal ultrasound is the first-line tool, while sonohysterography and MRI are recommended for specific cases.
3. **Screening for Coagulopathy**:
    
    - Coagulopathy screening is essential, especially for adolescents with heavy menstrual bleeding. Recommended tests include CBC, prothrombin time, and specific coagulation studies if indicated.
4. **Age-Related Etiologies**:
    
    - **Adolescents**: Commonly experience AUB from anovulation due to immature hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis.
    - **Ages 19–39**: Often related to pregnancy, polyps, PCOS, or hormonal contraception.
    - **Ages 40–Menopause**: Frequently due to anovulatory cycles, leiomyomas, or endometrial hyperplasia.
5. **Endometrial Sampling**:
    
    - Recommended for women over 45 or younger patients with high-risk factors (e.g., obesity, PCOS) or failed medical management.
6. **Management Recommendations**:
    
    - Medical management (e.g., NSAIDs, progestins, tranexamic acid) is often effective.
    - Surgical options (e.g., hysteroscopy, endometrial ablation) may be indicated for structural causes.

ACOG encourages adopting the PALM-COEIN system for standardized diagnosis, with personalized treatment based on etiology, patient age, and risk factors.
