The paper "Avoiding Inappropriate Clinical Decisions Based on False-Positive Human Chorionic Gonadotropin Test Results" from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) addresses the issue of false-positive hCG test results, which can lead to unnecessary clinical interventions. Key points include:

1. **Causes of False-Positive Results**: False positives can occur due to [[heterophile Ab]], which interact with animal-derived antigens used in hCG immunoassays. These antibodies are more common in people with exposure to animals, such as lab workers or those raised on farms.

2. **Detection and Verification**:
   - **Urine Tests**: Since heterophilic antibodies are not present in urine, a negative urine hCG test alongside a positive serum test can confirm interference.
   - **Serial Dilution**: Nonlinearity in diluted serum samples indicates interference.
   - **Alternate Assays**: Running the test with a different assay or preabsorbing the serum to remove interfering antibodies can confirm false-positive results.

3. **Clinical Management**: Physicians are advised to be cautious when laboratory results and clinical findings do not align. Immediate confirmation methods should be employed to avoid unnecessary treatments based on false-positive hCG results.

4. **Patient Counseling**: Patients with a history of assay interference should be informed of the risk for future false-positive results, and this should be documented in their medical records to prevent misdiagnosis.

The document underscores the importance of verifying hCG results when clinical discrepancies arise to prevent inappropriate interventions, such as unwarranted surgeries or chemotherapy.
