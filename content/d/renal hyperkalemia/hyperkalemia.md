"persistent" hyperkalemia
> [!tip] Condensed Description
> high [[K+]] in serum
> [[Sk]] > 5 mEq/L


> [!info] Etiology
> [[β1 blockers]]
> impaired excretion (<20mEq/day)
> [[hypoaldosteronism]] [[spironolactone]] [[RTA 4]]
> [[GFR]] < 15mL/min via [[AKI]] [[CKD]] [[NSAID]] [[trimethoprim]]

> [!warning] Pathogenesis
> mild: [[pacemaker cell]] enhanced automaticity by destruction of IKr current (6-6.5 mEq/L)
> severe: depolarization of [[neuron]] and [[cardiac muscle]] but somehow lowers excitability deactivating rapid inward [[Na+]]

> [!danger] Symptoms
> weakness
> [[respiratory failure]]
> [[tachyarrhythmia]] when mild
> [[sudden cardiac death]] when severe

> [!question] Objective Findings
> [[single void potassium-creatinine ratio]]
> [[EKG]] tall T wave; prolonged P, PR, and QRS; shortened QT interval

> [!abstract] Treatment
> [[calcium gluconate]] (stabilize [[cardiac muscle]])
> [[insulin]] + [[glucose]] or [[albuterol]] to lower [[K+]]
> 
> [[loop]] [[thiazide]]
> [[patiromer]]
> [[HD]] x2 if refractory for intracellular K+
> [[potassium binders]]

> [!example] Relationships
> [[translocational hyperkalemia]]
> [[metabolic acidosis]]
> [[spurious hyperkalemia]]
> make sure you don't think of [[monomorphic vtach]]
