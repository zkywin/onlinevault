Dear Dr. Daum, it was a pleasure to learn and work with you and the team for these four days. At the beginning of the rotation, I set goals for myself to commit to being thorough in the history and physical exam for patients, build rapport with patients, and to be a communicative/effective team player.

I feel my commitment to these goals was particularly reflected in my interactions with our patient with strictures, who needed to be asked many times to clarify on her history and needed to be told many times what her plan of care was. One challenge was in determining whether the patient’s relationship with food was primarily due to an eating disorder, or whether it was an anatomical problem. While it remains impossible for me to say for sure whether the chicken or the egg came first, I made sure to tell the patient’s side of the story so that we could at least consider it before taking the word “anorexia” at face value. She had many requests, ranging from a desire to attend an alcoholics anonymous meeting during her stay, to crushing pills so she could ingest the powder – I did my best to convey these in a manner that did not nag or distract the team from other important care considerations. And, although she would forget my words time and time again, I did feel that every time I recapitulated/clarified a part of the plan with her, she would feel at ease for at least a while longer.

I enjoyed that internal medicine is so abundant in opportunities for learning new things. I was excited to have the team’s support to apply ultrasound skills to check volume status on one of our patients (though regrettably, class schedule got in the way). This was a comfortable environment to learn alongside peers about topics like junctional rhythms, and to share learnings in on of my bite-sized presentations on strictures.

I have learned a great deal thanks to Sabrina, Julian, and Hannah - about CPRS’ specific documentation syntax/system, the shift handoff tool, and more – and I look forward to continue building skills that will be useful both now and down the line.
