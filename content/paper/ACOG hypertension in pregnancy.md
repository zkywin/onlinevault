The document "Chronic Hypertension in Pregnancy" is a detailed practice bulletin by the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, focusing on the definition, risks, management, and treatment of chronic hypertension during pregnancy. Below is a comprehensive summary:

---

### Key Points:

1. **Definition and Diagnosis**:
    
    - Chronic hypertension in pregnancy is hypertension diagnosed before pregnancy or within the first 20 weeks of gestation, or persisting postpartum.
    - Blood pressure is classified into stages: normal (<120/80 mmHg), elevated (120-129/<80 mmHg), Stage 1 (130-139/80-89 mmHg), and Stage 2 (≥140/90 mmHg).
    - Accurate diagnosis requires repeat measurements taken under standardized conditions.
2. **Prevalence and Trends**:
    
    - Affects 0.9–1.5% of pregnancies, with increasing rates due to maternal age and obesity.
    - Higher incidence in African American women.
3. **Maternal and Fetal Risks**:
    
    - **Maternal**: Increased risk of preeclampsia, cesarean delivery, stroke, renal dysfunction, and death.
    - **Fetal**: Preterm birth, growth restriction, stillbirth, placental abruption, and congenital anomalies (e.g., heart defects).
    - Superimposed preeclampsia complicates up to 50% of cases, leading to worse outcomes.
4. **Management Guidelines**:
    
    - **Prepregnancy**: Optimize maternal health, control blood pressure, and discontinue teratogenic antihypertensive drugs (e.g., ACE inhibitors).
    - **During Pregnancy**:
        - Start treatment if blood pressure is ≥160/110 mmHg or with end-organ damage.
        - Target blood pressure for treatment: 120–160 mmHg systolic and 80–110 mmHg diastolic.
        - Preferred medications include labetalol, nifedipine, and methyldopa; avoid ACE inhibitors and ARBs.
        - Low-dose aspirin (81 mg daily) should be initiated between 12–28 weeks to reduce the risk of preeclampsia.
5. **Monitoring and Evaluation**:
    
    - Perform baseline assessments, including renal function and proteinuria levels.
    - Regular fetal growth scans are recommended in the third trimester to monitor for growth restriction.
    - Continuous fetal monitoring during labor is advised for women with severe hypertension or complications.
6. **Intrapartum and Postpartum Care**:
    
    - Continue antihypertensive therapy during labor; neuraxial anesthesia is preferred.
    - Closely monitor blood pressure postpartum, as hypertensive disorders may persist or worsen.
7. **Special Considerations**:
    
    - Superimposed preeclampsia can present diagnostic challenges; it is identified by new or worsening proteinuria, thrombocytopenia, or liver dysfunction in women with pre-existing hypertension.
    - White coat hypertension requires careful monitoring but is associated with lower risks.
8. **Emergencies**:
    
    - Severe acute hypertension requires prompt treatment with agents like IV labetalol, hydralazine, or oral nifedipine to prevent complications like stroke or organ failure.
