The paper "Early Pregnancy Loss" is a practice bulletin from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) providing updated clinical management guidelines for obstetricians and gynecologists. It covers the diagnosis, etiology, and treatment options for early pregnancy loss, defined as the loss of an intrauterine pregnancy within the first trimester. Key points include:

1. **Diagnosis**: Early pregnancy loss is diagnosed through clinical symptoms (e.g., bleeding and cramping), ultrasonography, and serum β-hCG testing. Specific ultrasound criteria help distinguish viable from nonviable pregnancies.

2. **Etiology and Risk Factors**: Chromosomal abnormalities cause about 50% of early pregnancy losses, with risk factors including advanced maternal age and prior pregnancy loss.

3. **Management Options**: Three main approaches are described:
   - **Expectant Management**: Allows natural expulsion, effective in about 80% of cases, typically for women without immediate complications.
   - **Medical Management**: Misoprostol alone, or combined with mifepristone, shortens the time to expulsion. This method is effective and reduces the need for surgical intervention.
   - **Surgical Management**: Uterine evacuation, typically with suction curettage, is recommended in cases of hemorrhage, infection, or patient preference for immediate resolution.

4. **Complications**: Infections, hemorrhage, and uterine adhesions are rare across all management approaches. The choice of intervention should be guided by patient preference and clinical indications.

5. **Patient Counseling**: Counseling includes options for contraception, guidance on interpregnancy intervals, and RhD alloimmunization prevention for Rh-negative women.

6. **Cost Considerations**: Surgical management in an office setting is generally more cost-effective than medical management or surgical intervention in an operating room.

The bulletin underscores the importance of individualized treatment, shared decision-making, and thorough counseling on the risks and benefits of each management option.
