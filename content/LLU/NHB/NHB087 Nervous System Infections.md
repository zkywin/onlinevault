# Case 1 [[meningococcus]]
One week after arriving at the ==Army recruit== camp at Fort Ord, California, a 19-year-old recruit becomes the first of three cases of meningitis. He has the precipitous onset of fever and headache and feels pain in his neck when he moves his head. When he arrived at the infirmary, he had a ==petechial rash== on his lower extremities. On lumbar puncture, his opening pressure is slightly elevated at 22 cm H2O (normal range is <20 cm H2O). His CSF has 1,100/mm3 white blood cells (WBCs; 80% neutrophils), 29 mg/dL glucose, and 100 mg/dL protein. 

# Case 2 [[HSV-1]]
A 60-year-old man is brought to the hospital with confusion, right-sided weakness, and fever. His daughter reports that when she arrived at his house he thought “there were devils in the room”. On route to the hospital, he hallucinated intermittently, saying that he smelled roses. Three days previously, he had complained of mild nausea and vomited once. Upon arrival at the Emergency Department (ED), he has a generalized seizure. A computed tomography (CT) scan of his head is normal, but a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan documents T1-hypointense and T2-hyperintense signals in the cortex and gray-white matter junction in the ==left temporal lobe==. CSF obtained by lumbar puncture contains 50 WBCs/mm3 (90% lymphocytes) and 300 RBCs/mm3, 70 mg/dL glucose, and 100 mg/dL protein. 

# Case 3 [[strep pneumoniae]] [[MSSA]]
A 29-year-old woman complains of an earache. She has had many such episodes in the past, especially involving the right ear, but the most recent episode was associated with a headache that made her nauseated. She vomited once. After four days of those symptoms, she was driven to the hospital because she had developed a large blind spot in her left field of vision. At the hospital, she has a temperature of 100.6˚F (38.1˚C). A CT scan reveals a 4×3- cm mass in her right occipital lobe, consistent with an abscess (Fig. 1). A neurosurgeon performs a needle aspiration of the abscess under CT guidance. A smear of the aspirate reveals Gram-positive cocci and Gram-negative bacilli. The abscess fluid is sent for culture under both aerobic and anaerobic conditions. She is empirically treated with broad-spectrum antibiotics while awaiting the results of the culture.

# Case 4 [[west nile virus]]
A 72-year-old woman is hospitalized in Illinois in late August with a high fever and altered mental status. Previously she was healthy, but has for the last several days experienced fever, headache, cognitive decline, and difficulty walking. She is active outdoors, president of the local garden club, has not traveled out of the area, and her husband is not ill. On admission, her temperature is 103.1˚F (39.5˚C), and her heart rate is 120 bpm. On exam, she is disoriented, and her left leg is weak. Her CSF shows 150 WBCs/mm3 (75% lymphocytes), 100 mg/dL glucose, and 90 mg/dL protein. Antibiotics are given but she becomes increasingly obtunded. No bacteria are isolated from her blood or CSF. Five days after admission, she has two generalized seizures, becomes deeply comatose, and requires mechanical ventilation. Muscle tone is flaccid, and deep tendon reflexes are absent. She shows no signs of improvement over the next 2 weeks, and life support is withdrawn on day 19 of hospitalization. CSF and serum samples are positive for IgM antibody to an infectious agent.
# Case 5 [[rabies]]
A 55-year-old avid outdoorsman residing in Texas County, Missouri, was bitten on the left ear by a bat after allowing it to crawl up his arm and neck. He did not obtain medical care for the bite immediately following the injury. Five weeks later, he goes to the local ED complaining of numbness and pruritus of the left ear and face, headache, chest pain, and dysphagia. He is hospitalized and treated, but over the next few days, his symptoms progress, and he shows steady neurological decline. He becomes febrile, confused, and disoriented, and dies 7 days after admission.

# Case 6 [[listeria]]
A 64-year-old woman is brought to the ED after 5 days of fever, headache and confusion. A few days before her present symptoms began, she had diarrhea for 2 days that resolved without treatment. She has a history of rheumatoid arthritis for which she has been taking ==prednisone== daily for the past several months. On exam, she has a temperature of 101.8˚F (38.8˚C), a pulse of 102/min, a respiration rate of 18/min and BP of 134/78 mm Hg. She is unable to answer simple questions and appears agitated, but there is no nuchal rigidity nor focal neurologic deficits. Her CSF has 620 WBCs/mm3 (66% neutrophils & 34% lymphocytes), 40 mg/dL glucose and 175 mg/dL protein. A Gram stain of the CSF reveals a moderate number of neutrophils, but no organisms; however cultures of both the CSF and blood grow a significant pathogen that has tumbling motility and is able to grow at 4˚C.
# Case 7 [[echovirus]] [[enterovirus D68]]
During a 4-week period in August in a rural county in the Midwest, a total of 29 persons between the ages of 9 and 15 years have a rapid onset of fever, headache, stiff neck and photophobia. Some patients had diarrhea for a few days preceding the headache. Patient Q, who lives in this town, is an illappearing 12-year-old boy in moderate distress due to a headache. He has some ==photophobia== and mild nuchal rigidity, but has a negative Brudzinski sign (flexion of the hip and knee when the neck is passively flexed). His vital signs include a temperature of 102.6˚F (39.2˚C), pulse of 112/min, respiration rate of 16/ min, and blood pressure of 130/84 mmHg. His CSF has 54 WBCs/mm3 (92% lymphocytes), 65 mg/dL glucose and 50 mg/dL protein.
# Case 8 [[la crosse virus]]
A 13-year-old boy presents with 3 days of vomiting, fever to 101.3˚F (38.5˚C), a severe headache, agitation and confusion. Two weeks previously he visited the North Carolina mountains, where he spent several days hiking. On physical examination, his vital signs are normal, his neck is supple, and he does not have lymphadenopathy. His arms and legs have dozens of ==mosquito== bite marks. A lumbar puncture reveals 198 WBCs/mm3 (80% lymphocytes), 71 mg/dL glucose and 55 mg/dL protein. Herpes simplex virus (HSV) PCR is negative, as is Rocky Mountain Spotted Fever and Lyme disease serologic testing. A bacterial Gram stain and culture are also negative. During hospitalization his mental status deteriorates, his heart rate drops to 30 to 40 beats/min, and he is ==intubated== in the pediatric intensive care unit. Head CT and MRI imaging scans show no evidence of increased intracranial pressure. He gradually improves and is discharged after 7 days of hospitalization when his mental status returns to baseline. A serologic test reveals the etiologic agent of the disease.
# Case 9 [[toxoplasmosis]]
A 30-year-old woman is brought to the ED with a 2-week history of progressively severe headache, nausea and vomiting; several seizures have occurred over the past 2 days. She has been HIV positive for 3 years and was diagnosed with AIDS a year before the current episode. She has been on HIV therapy, but is currently failing her regime. Her brother can’t recall any history of seizures. She appears in moderate distress from the headache, but is able to answer questions. Mild right-sided weakness is evident and an MRI reveals ring-enhancing lesions in both the left parietal and right frontal lobes (Fig. 2).
# Case 10 [[cryptococcal meningitis]]
A 45-year-old man is brought to the ED because of fever, severe headache, nausea, vomiting and mental status change that has been progressing over the past 2 weeks. The patient was diagnosed with HIV two years ago and is not currently on antiretroviral therapy. On exam he has a temperature of 101.3˚F (38.5˚C), pulse of 106/min, respiration rate of 18/min, and BP of 110/62 mmHg. The patient is lethargic and disoriented. Nuchal rigidity is noted as well as a positive Brudzinski sign. CSF: 80 WBCs/ mm3 (32% neutrophils & 66% lymphocytes), protein 68 mg/dL, and glucose 40 mg/dL. An India ink prep of the CSF is diagnostic for the causative agent.
# Case 11 [[pork tapeworm]]
A 28-year-old Hispanic man is brought to the ED for severe headaches and two generalized seizures. Several weeks ago, he began having headaches that have now become more frequent. He denies fevers or chills. He is a recent immigrant from Mexico, but there is no previous history of seizures. His CSF reveals 35 WBCs/mm3, with lymphocytic and eosinophilic pleocytosis (increased cell count), protein of 62 mg/dL, and glucose of 60 mg/dL. A CT scan of his brain reveals an intracranial calcified cyst, and further imaging with MRI confirms the presence of similar lesions, some with scolex visible
# Case 12 [[botulism]]
A 39-year-old man with a history of injection drug use presents to the ED with slurred speech, diplopia and dysphagia. A physical exam shows bilateral ptosis (eyelid drooping), a sluggish pupillary response to light, bilateral 6th-cranial nerve palsies (affecting vision in both eyes), and multiple skin abscesses on his arms and legs. Shortly after admission, the dysphagia progresses, necessitating intubation for airway protection. Electromyography suggests a disorder of neuromuscular transmission and the causative agent is cultured from an abscess specimen.
