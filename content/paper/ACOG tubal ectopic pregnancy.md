The paper titled "Tubal Ectopic Pregnancy" from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) discusses the guidelines for diagnosing and managing ectopic pregnancies, particularly those in the fallopian tubes, which constitute more than 90% of cases. Key points include:

1. **Definition and Prevalence**: An ectopic pregnancy occurs outside the uterus, with most cases in the fallopian tube. It remains a significant cause of pregnancy-related mortality despite improved diagnostic methods.

2. **Risk Factors**: Factors like previous ectopic pregnancies, tubal damage, assisted reproductive technology, and age over 35 increase risk, though half of cases present with no identifiable risk factors.

3. **Diagnosis**: Transvaginal ultrasound and serial hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) measurements are essential in diagnosing ectopic pregnancies. The "discriminatory hCG level" helps gauge whether an intrauterine pregnancy should be visible on ultrasound; levels above this threshold without an intrauterine pregnancy suggest ectopic pregnancy.

4. **Management**: Treatment depends on stability and preference:
   - **Methotrexate Therapy**: Medical management with methotrexate is effective in stable patients without contraindications and can avoid surgery.
   - **Surgical Management**: Required if the ectopic pregnancy is ruptured or if the patient is hemodynamically unstable. Surgical options include salpingectomy or salpingostomy, with the choice guided by tubal damage and fertility desires.
   - **Expectant Management**: In cases of low, declining hCG and no symptoms, monitoring may be appropriate.

5. **Follow-Up**: Serial hCG levels are monitored post-treatment to ensure resolution. Methotrexate regimens vary (single-dose, two-dose, or multiple-dose) based on hCG levels and patient response.

The document emphasizes personalized care, taking into account patient stability, preferences, and fertility goals to determine the optimal management approach for ectopic pregnancies.
