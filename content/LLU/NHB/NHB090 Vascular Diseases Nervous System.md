> posterior circulation strokes will have "crossed findings" ipsilateral cranial nerve problems and contralateral arm and leg problems
> anterior circulation strokes will have contralateral for both


[[TIA]] lasts for minutes and [[ischemic stroke]] lasts for much longer. Large vessel strokes include [[anterior stroke]] and [[posterior stroke]]. Unlike the large vessel strokes that are usually combinations of losses, [[lacunar stroke]] is usually isolated. [[watershed stroke]] will not directly attack key areas, but the so-called watershed areas can still create impairment of proximal muscles and vision.

Intracerebral hemorrhage is caused by rupture of [[d/charcot-bouchard aneurysms]]. [[putaminal hemorrhage]] is most common, while [[d/thalamic hemorrhage]] and [[d/pontine hemorrhage]] are much more deadly. [[d/cerebellar hemorrhage]] is the most treatable and can be life-saving. Hemorrhage can also be caused by [[cerebral AV malformation]], medications like [[warfarin]] and [[tPA]], or even follow a thrombotic stroke. In [[alzheimer's]] patients, [[cerebral amyloid angiopathy]] is another risk.

[[left subarachnoid hemorrhage]] is caused by rupture of [[berry aneurysm]], most of the time in the anterior circulation. Meanwhile, [[CVT]] mostly occurs at the [[a/superior sagital sinus]] and causes damage in all the areas surrounding that midline structure. #done
